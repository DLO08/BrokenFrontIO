# Trade

A tab on the left edge of the screen opens the **Trade** panel. It holds four
things: trade agreements, your tariff, resource offers, and the world uranium
market.

## Trade agreements

A bilateral deal, separate from an alliance. Propose one from the trade panel
(or accept the request that appears there); it lasts **10 minutes**, a pending
request expires after 20s, and signing one raises how both sides feel about
each other.

While an agreement stands, trade ships between your ports and theirs pay
**25% more gold to both sides** — not a split, a bonus to each. Attacking a
partner ends the agreement, as it ends trade generally.

An agreement is not an alliance: partners can still attack each other, and
neither side is marked a traitor for ending it.

## Tariffs

Your tariff is a levy on trade ships arriving at **your** ports from players
you have no agreement with. You can set it to 0%, 10% or 25%.

| Situation | Who gets what |
| --- | --- |
| Partners | Both sides get the payout +25% |
| No agreement | The destination's tariff moves that share of the payout from the sender to the receiver |

A tariff takes gold from your trading partners' pockets rather than creating
it, so a high tariff is an incentive for them to sign an agreement instead —
or to stop trading with you.

## Offers

A direct swap with any player you can trade with: offer gold and uranium, ask
for gold and uranium. The recipient has **30s** to accept, only the recipient
can accept (either side can decline), and the swap is all-or-nothing — if
either side can't cover their end when it is accepted, nothing moves. One open
offer per pair, per direction.

This is how uranium actually changes hands between players who aren't allies:
sell it at the price you choose rather than the market's.

## The world uranium market

Buy and sell uranium for gold at any time, with no counterparty.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Base price | 60,000 gold per uranium |
| Spread | Buy at +10%, sell at −10% |
| Price moves | +1.5% per unit of net demand |
| Range | 20,000 – 300,000 gold |
| Decay | Net demand drifts back toward zero over time |

The price is global and shared: every purchase pushes it up for everyone, every
sale pushes it down, and it settles back if the world stops trading. A player
with spare deposits can mine for export; a player with gold and no deposits can
buy their way to a warhead, at a price that rises the more everyone does it.

The market needs the uranium economy on; agreements, tariffs and offers need
trade deals on. See [the feature list](#modal=wiki&page=index).
